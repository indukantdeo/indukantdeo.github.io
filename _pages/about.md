## Research background

At Corning, I examine how network and hardware choices affect training efficiency and inference latency, including time to first token and time per output token.

I completed my Ph.D. in Mechanical Engineering at the University of British Columbia in 2025, where I worked with [Prof. Rajeev Jaiman](https://mech.ubc.ca/rajeev-jaiman/) in the [Computational Multiphysics Laboratory](https://cml.mech.ubc.ca/). My [dissertation](https://doi.org/10.14288/1.0449988) focused on physics-guided deep learning for fluid flow and ocean acoustics.

My research projects include the following:

1. Building a convolutional autoencoder and sequence-to-sequence attention network for long-time-horizon wave propagation predictions, published in Physics of Fluids.
2. Developing a space-time reduced order model that combines 3D convolution kernels to efficiently predict fluid flow dynamics, published in Physics of Fluids and **selected as Editor's pick.**
3. Introducing a **finite element-inspired hypergraph neural network** for fluid dynamics simulations, published in the **Journal of Computational Physics**.
4. Developing [continual learning models](https://doi.org/10.1121/10.0036773) for underwater transmission loss and [probabilistic models](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.oceaneng.2026.125461) that estimate uncertainty in acoustic predictions.
5. Applying parameterized reduced-order modeling techniques to predict distortion in metal 3D printing, which led to a publication in the NeurIPS Machine Learning for Physical Sciences Workshop.
6. Building an optimization framework which reduces the impact of shipping noise on marine mammals, published in Ocean Engineering journal.

Outside work, I enjoy hiking, climbing, skiing, and swimming in open water.
